Critical Alert

Identify Fraud Messages

Learn to spot fake messages before they trick you. Think twice before clicking or sharing personal information.

Urgency or Fear Tactics

Fraud messages often create panic with phrases like "Account blocked today!" or "Immediate action required."

Example:

"Your bank account will be suspended in 2 hours. Click here to verify immediately!"

High Risk

Suspicious Links

Shortened URLs or links that don't match the official company website are red flags.

Example:

"bit.ly/bank-update" instead of "yourbank.com/update"

Very High Risk

Personal Information Requests

Legitimate organizations never ask for OTP, PIN, CVV, or passwords via message.

Example:

"Share your OTP to verify your account. We need it for security."

Critical Risk

Grammar & Spelling Errors

Many scam messages contain poor spelling, awkward language, or strange formatting.

Example:

"Congratulation! You have won prize. Send detail for claim."

Medium Risk

Too-Good-To-Be-True Offers

Messages claiming lottery wins, free gifts, or unbelievable discounts are usually scams.

Example:

"You won ₹10,00,000! Click to claim your prize money now!"

High Risk

Unknown or Fake Senders

Messages from unfamiliar numbers pretending to be banks, companies, or government agencies.

Example:

"This is Income Tax Department. Your PAN will be blocked. Reply immediately."

High Risk

Safety First - Always Remember

Never share OTP, PIN, or passwords with anyone. Banks never ask for these details via messages or calls. Always verify the sender's identity independently. When in doubt, don't click, call the official number instead.
